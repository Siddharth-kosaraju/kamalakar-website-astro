## Warning Signs of Heart Problems

Consult a cardiologist if you experience:

- Chest pain, pressure or heaviness
- Shortness of breath
- Pain spreading to the arm, neck or jaw
- Dizziness or fainting
- Fast or irregular heartbeat
- Excessive sweating
- Persistent tiredness
- Swelling in the feet or legs

Severe chest pain, sudden breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw may be signs of a heart attack. **Seek emergency medical care immediately.** Our guide on [what to do during a medical emergency](/blog/medical-emergency-heart-attack-first-aid/) explains the first steps in detail.

## Heart Treatment Options

### Medicines and Lifestyle Changes

Some heart problems can be managed through prescribed medicines, a [healthy diet](/blog/best-food-habits-for-a-healthy-heart/), regular activity and better control of diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol.

### Angiogram and Angioplasty

An angiogram helps identify blockages in the heart arteries. If a serious blockage is found, [angioplasty](/services/angioplasty/) may be recommended to improve blood flow. A stent may also be placed to keep the artery open.

### Pacemaker Treatment

A [pacemaker](/services/pacemaker/) may be advised for patients with a slow or irregular heartbeat.

### Heart Failure Management

[Heart failure treatment](/services/heart-failure/) may include medicines, diet guidance, regular monitoring and lifestyle changes to improve heart function.

### Emergency Cardiac Care

Heart attacks require immediate treatment. Kamalakar Heart Centre provides [emergency cardiac evaluation](/services/emergency-cardiac-care/) and treatment based on the patient's medical condition.

### When should I see a cardiologist?

See a cardiologist if you have chest pain or pressure, breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness or fainting, unusual tiredness, or swelling in the feet. You should also consult if you have diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol or a family history of heart disease — even without symptoms.

### Do I need an angiogram for chest pain?

Not always. Most patients start with an ECG, 2D Echo or treadmill test. An [angiogram](/blog/angiography-procedure-preparation-risks-recovery/) is recommended only when those tests or your symptoms suggest a significant blockage that needs to be confirmed and located.
