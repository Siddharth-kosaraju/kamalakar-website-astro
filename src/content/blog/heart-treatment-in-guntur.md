Heart problems can develop without clear warning signs. Chest pain, breathing difficulty, unusual tiredness or an irregular heartbeat should never be ignored. Early diagnosis and timely treatment can help prevent serious complications.

This guide explains how **heart treatment in Guntur** typically proceeds, and lists the services available at [Kamalakar Heart Centre](/services/).

## Heart Conditions That Need Treatment

Every heart condition needs careful evaluation. Some patients may require medicines and lifestyle changes, while others may need angioplasty, pacemaker treatment or emergency cardiac care.

Common conditions treated by a cardiologist include:

- Chest pain and breathing difficulty
- Coronary artery disease
- Heart attack
- [Heart failure](/services/heart-failure/)
- [High blood pressure](/services/hypertension-cholesterol/)
- High cholesterol
- Irregular heartbeat
- Heart blockage
- Slow heart rate

Treatment is planned based on the patient's symptoms, medical history and overall health.

## Warning Signs of Heart Problems

Consult a cardiologist if you experience:

- Chest pain, pressure or heaviness
- Shortness of breath
- Pain spreading to the arm, neck or jaw
- Dizziness or fainting
- Fast or irregular heartbeat
- Excessive sweating
- Persistent tiredness
- Swelling in the feet or legs

Severe chest pain, sudden breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw may be signs of a heart attack. **Seek emergency medical care immediately.** Our guide on [what to do during a medical emergency](/blog/medical-emergency-heart-attack-first-aid/) explains the first steps in detail.

## Heart Tests and Diagnosis

To understand the cause of a heart problem, the cardiologist may recommend:

- [ECG](/services/ecg-echo/)
- 2D Echo
- Treadmill test
- Blood tests
- Blood pressure evaluation
- [Coronary angiogram](/blog/angiography-procedure-preparation-risks-recovery/)

Not every patient needs all these tests. The required investigations are selected based on the symptoms and medical condition.

## Heart Treatment Options

### Medicines and Lifestyle Changes

Some heart problems can be managed through prescribed medicines, a [healthy diet](/blog/best-food-habits-for-a-healthy-heart/), regular activity and better control of diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol.

### Angiogram and Angioplasty

An angiogram helps identify blockages in the heart arteries. If a serious blockage is found, [angioplasty](/services/angioplasty/) may be recommended to improve blood flow. A stent may also be placed to keep the artery open.

### Pacemaker Treatment

A [pacemaker](/services/pacemaker/) may be advised for patients with a slow or irregular heartbeat.

### Heart Failure Management

[Heart failure treatment](/services/heart-failure/) may include medicines, diet guidance, regular monitoring and lifestyle changes to improve heart function.

### Emergency Cardiac Care

Heart attacks require immediate treatment. Call 108 or go to the nearest emergency department; [emergency cardiac evaluation](/services/emergency-cardiac-care/) and treatment depend on the patient's medical condition.

### When should I see a cardiologist?

See a cardiologist if you have chest pain or pressure, breathlessness, palpitations, dizziness or fainting, unusual tiredness, or swelling in the feet. If you have diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol or a family history of heart disease, ask your doctor whether a heart check-up is advisable even without symptoms.

### Do I need an angiogram for chest pain?

Not always. Most patients start with an ECG, 2D Echo or treadmill test. An [angiogram](/blog/angiography-procedure-preparation-risks-recovery/) is recommended only when those tests or your symptoms suggest a significant blockage that needs to be confirmed and located.

### Does Kamalakar Heart Centre handle heart emergencies?

Yes. The centre provides [emergency cardiac care](/services/emergency-cardiac-care/), including evaluation and treatment for suspected heart attacks. For a suspected heart attack, call for emergency help immediately rather than travelling to the clinic unassisted.
