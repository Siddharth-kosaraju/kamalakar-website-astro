Have you ever experienced a burning sensation in your chest after eating and assumed it was acidity? Many people experience chest discomfort and immediately blame gas, indigestion or acid reflux. While these are common causes of discomfort, sometimes similar symptoms can be associated with a serious heart problem.

A heart attack does not always begin with severe chest pain. Some people experience pressure, burning, breathlessness, nausea or discomfort that feels like indigestion. Mistaking these warning signs for acidity can delay urgent medical treatment.

So how can you tell the difference between a heart attack and acidity? Understanding the symptoms can help you make safer decisions about your health.

## What Is the Difference Between a Heart Attack and Acidity?

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to part of the heart muscle becomes severely reduced or blocked. This usually happens because of a problem in the coronary arteries that supply blood to the heart — see [what causes heart blockages](/blog/what-causes-heart-blockages-risk-factors/).

Acidity, commonly associated with acid reflux, occurs when stomach contents flow back into the food pipe. This can cause a burning sensation in the chest, a sour taste in the mouth or discomfort after meals.

Although these conditions affect different parts of the body, their symptoms can sometimes feel similar.

The important point is that **you cannot reliably distinguish a heart attack from acidity based on symptoms alone.** Medical assessment may be needed to identify the cause.

## Heart Attack vs. Acidity: Key Differences

Understanding common symptom patterns can help you recognise when medical attention is needed. However, these differences are not reliable enough to rule out a heart attack at home.

### 1. How the Discomfort Feels

**Acidity or acid reflux**

- A burning sensation behind the breastbone
- A sour or bitter taste in the mouth
- Discomfort that may occur after meals
- Symptoms that may worsen when lying down or bending over

**Heart attack**

- Pressure, tightness, squeezing or heaviness in the chest
- Discomfort that may spread to the arms, back, neck or jaw
- A feeling of fullness or burning that resembles indigestion
- Discomfort that may last several minutes or disappear and return

Not every heart attack causes severe pain. Some people experience mild or unusual symptoms that are easy to overlook.

### 2. When Symptoms Occur

Acidity often develops after eating a large meal, eating late at night or lying down soon after a meal.

Heart-related discomfort may occur during physical activity, emotional stress or even at rest. However, a heart attack can happen at any time, including after eating.

**Do not assume chest discomfort is acidity simply because it started after a meal.**

### 3. Other Symptoms to Watch For

Acidity may cause bloating, belching and a sour taste in the mouth.

A heart attack may also cause:

- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweating
- Nausea or vomiting
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Unusual weakness or fatigue
- Pain or discomfort in the arm, shoulder, back, neck or jaw

Some people experience only a few of these symptoms. Others may not have obvious chest pain. Read more about the [7 warning signs of a heart attack](/blog/7-warning-signs-heart-attack-never-ignore/).

### 4. Does Relief After an Antacid Mean It Is Not a Heart Attack?

No. Improvement after taking an antacid does not reliably rule out a heart problem. Symptoms can fluctuate, and some heart-related discomfort may temporarily improve.

If you have new or unexplained chest discomfort, especially with breathlessness, sweating or pain spreading to other parts of the body, seek urgent medical assessment rather than repeatedly taking acidity medicines.

## Can a Heart Attack Feel Like Gas or Indigestion?

Yes. A heart attack can cause discomfort that feels like indigestion, heartburn or upper abdominal pain.

This can happen because pain from the heart and discomfort from the digestive system may feel similar. The symptoms may be particularly confusing when there is no dramatic chest pain.

People with diabetes and older adults may sometimes experience less typical symptoms. Women can also experience nausea, unusual fatigue, breathlessness or discomfort in the back, shoulder or jaw alongside or instead of prominent chest pain — see [heart attack symptoms in women](/blog/heart-attack-symptoms-in-women/).

It is important to remember that heart attacks can affect people of different ages. A person should not dismiss concerning symptoms simply because they appear young or otherwise healthy.

## Who Has a Higher Risk of Heart Attack?

Some factors increase the likelihood of coronary artery disease and heart attack.

These include:

- High blood pressure
- Diabetes
- High LDL cholesterol
- Smoking or tobacco use
- A family history of premature heart disease
- Excess body weight and physical inactivity
- Existing coronary artery disease

Having one or more risk factors does not mean that every episode of chest discomfort is a heart attack. However, these factors make it especially important to take new or unexplained symptoms seriously. If you have diabetes or high blood pressure, read [how they affect your heart](/blog/diabetes-high-bp-heart-disease-risk/).

Even people without known risk factors can experience a heart attack.

## How Do Doctors Diagnose a Heart Attack?

Doctors do not rely on symptoms alone to determine whether chest discomfort is caused by acidity or a heart problem.

Depending on the symptoms and clinical findings, the evaluation may include:

**Electrocardiogram (ECG):** Records the electrical activity of the heart and can identify changes that suggest a heart attack. More on the [ECG and 2D Echo](/services/ecg-echo/).

**High-sensitivity troponin blood test:** Measures a protein released when heart muscle cells are damaged. Repeat testing may be needed depending on when symptoms started and the initial results.

**Other investigations:** Depending on the situation, a doctor may recommend an echocardiogram, coronary CT imaging or [coronary angiography](/blog/angiography-procedure-preparation-risks-recovery/).

A normal initial ECG does not always exclude a heart attack. If symptoms remain concerning, further assessment and repeat testing may be necessary.

The appropriate tests depend on the patient's condition and the doctor's assessment.

## What Should You Do If You Suspect a Heart Attack?

If you or someone nearby experiences new chest pressure, tightness or burning with breathlessness, cold sweating, faintness or discomfort spreading to the arm, back or jaw, treat it as a possible medical emergency.

1. Call emergency services immediately (112 or 108 in India).
2. Stop physical activity and sit down while waiting for help.
3. Do not drive yourself to the hospital.
4. Follow the emergency dispatcher's instructions.
5. Do not delay medical care to try antacids or other home remedies.

Do not wait for symptoms to become severe or assume they will disappear on their own. Prompt medical treatment can reduce heart muscle damage and improve outcomes. Our [heart attack first-aid guide](/blog/medical-emergency-heart-attack-first-aid/) explains what to do while help is on the way.

## When Should You Consult a Cardiologist?

Arrange a medical assessment for recurrent or unexplained chest discomfort, particularly if it occurs during physical activity or you have risk factors such as diabetes, high blood pressure or high cholesterol.

However, if you have symptoms suggesting an ongoing heart attack, seek emergency care immediately rather than waiting for a routine cardiology appointment.

Early assessment can help identify the cause of symptoms and determine whether further investigation or treatment is necessary.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can I tell if chest pain is acidity or a heart attack?

Acidity may cause burning discomfort, a sour taste and symptoms after meals. A heart attack may cause pressure, tightness, burning or discomfort that spreads to other areas. Because the symptoms overlap, you cannot safely rule out a heart attack based on how the pain feels.

### Can gas cause chest pain on the left side?

Gas and digestive problems can cause abdominal or chest discomfort. However, pain on the left side of the chest can also have heart-related causes. New, unexplained or severe chest pain should be assessed promptly.

### Can a heart attack occur without chest pain?

Yes. Some people experience breathlessness, nausea, cold sweating, unusual fatigue or discomfort in the back, jaw or upper abdomen without prominent chest pain.

### Can acidity cause pain in the left arm?

Acidity typically causes symptoms related to the chest and food pipe. Pain spreading to the arm can be a warning sign of a heart problem. If this occurs with chest discomfort or other concerning symptoms, seek emergency medical attention.

### Does a normal ECG mean my heart is healthy?

Not necessarily. An ECG provides important information about the heart's electrical activity, but it cannot detect every heart problem. Your doctor may recommend blood tests or other investigations based on your symptoms and risk factors.

### When should I go to the emergency department for chest pain?

Seek emergency care for new or unexplained chest pressure, tightness or burning, particularly when accompanied by breathlessness, sweating, nausea, faintness or pain spreading to the arm, back, neck or jaw. Do not wait to see whether the symptoms improve.

## Conclusion

Acidity and heart attacks can produce similar symptoms, making it difficult to identify the cause without medical assessment. While acidity is common, assuming that every episode of chest burning or discomfort is digestive can be dangerous.

Pay attention to new or unusual symptoms, understand your heart disease risk factors and seek medical help when something does not feel right. When a heart attack is possible, emergency assessment is the safest course of action.
