Recovering from open heart surgery takes time, patience, and the right guidance. **Cardiac rehabilitation exercises** can help improve strength, stamina, circulation, and confidence as you gradually return to your normal routine.

Exercise after heart surgery should always be started **only with your cardiologist or cardiac rehabilitation team's approval**. Recovery is different for every patient, so never rush or compare your progress with others.

## Why Is Exercise Important After Heart Surgery?

Gentle, regular exercise can help:

- Improve heart and lung fitness
- Increase energy and stamina
- Improve blood circulation
- Strengthen muscles
- Reduce stiffness
- Support a safer return to daily activities
- Improve confidence during recovery

Whether your surgery was a bypass operation or a valve repair, the principles are the same. Patients who had a stent placed through [coronary angioplasty](/services/angioplasty/) rather than open surgery usually recover faster, but the same "start slow, build gradually" approach applies.

Here are some commonly recommended exercises that may be introduced gradually during cardiac rehabilitation.

## 1. Gentle Walking

Walking is usually one of the easiest exercises to begin after open heart surgery.

Start with short, comfortable walks around your home, hospital corridor, or outdoors on a flat surface. Gradually increase the duration as your strength improves.

**Tip:** Begin slowly and avoid walking until you feel exhausted.

## 2. Ankle Pumps

Sit comfortably or lie down with your legs relaxed.

Slowly point your toes away from you, then bring them back toward your body. Repeat gently with both feet.

This simple movement helps keep your legs active and supports healthy circulation.

## 3. Seated Marching

Sit upright on a stable chair with both feet on the floor.

Slowly lift one knee, lower it, and then lift the other knee as though you are marching while seated.

Seated marching can help improve leg strength, mobility, and endurance.

## 4. Deep Breathing Exercises

Sit comfortably and relax your shoulders.

Take a slow, deep breath through your nose and gently breathe out through your mouth.

Deep breathing exercises can support lung function and help you relax during the recovery period.

## 5. Sit-to-Stand Exercise

<figure class="not-prose my-6">
<picture>
<source srcset="/media/blog-cardiac-rehab-sit-to-stand.webp" type="image/webp" />

When your doctor says it is safe, sit on a stable chair with your feet firmly on the floor.

Slowly stand up and then gently return to the seated position.

This exercise can strengthen the legs and make everyday activities such as getting up from a chair easier.

Patients whose breastbone was opened during surgery should follow their surgeon's instructions about using their arms for support.

## 6. Stationary Cycling

As your recovery progresses, your cardiac rehabilitation team may recommend gentle stationary cycling.

Start at a low resistance and comfortable pace. Increase the duration gradually only when advised by your healthcare team.

Stationary cycling can help improve cardiovascular fitness and stamina.

## Important Safety Tips

During recovery:

- Start slowly and increase activity gradually.
- Warm up before exercise and cool down afterward.
- Avoid heavy lifting or strenuous upper-body exercise until your doctor approves it.
- Take regular breaks when needed.
- Stay hydrated — this matters even more during [Guntur's hot summer months](/blog/summer-heat-heart-health-tips/).
- Follow your prescribed cardiac rehabilitation plan.

### Stop Exercising and Contact Your Doctor If You Experience:

- Chest pain or pressure
- Severe or unusual shortness of breath
- Dizziness or faintness
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Unusual weakness
- Pain or discomfort around the surgical area

If chest pain does not settle within a few minutes of rest, treat it as an emergency — call **112 or 108** for an ambulance, or go straight to an [emergency cardiac care](/services/emergency-cardiac-care/) facility. Our guide to [heart attack first aid](/blog/medical-emergency-heart-attack-first-aid/) explains what to do while you wait.

## Take Recovery One Step at a Time

Cardiac rehabilitation is not about exercising harder. It is about **helping your heart and body become stronger gradually and safely**.

With the right medical guidance, regular activity, and patience, many patients can steadily regain their strength and return to a more active lifestyle after open heart surgery. Pair your exercise plan with [heart-healthy eating](/blog/best-food-habits-for-a-healthy-heart/) and good control of [blood pressure and cholesterol](/services/hypertension-cholesterol/) for the best long-term results.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When can I start exercising after open heart surgery?

Most patients begin very gentle movement — ankle pumps, deep breathing and short walks — within days of surgery, while still in hospital. Structured cardiac rehabilitation usually starts a few weeks later, once your surgeon and cardiologist confirm the breastbone and incision are healing well. Never start a new exercise without their approval.

### How far should I walk after heart surgery?

Start with a few minutes at a time on a flat surface and increase the duration gradually as your stamina returns. The goal is to feel comfortable, not exhausted. Your cardiac rehabilitation team will set targets that suit your age, surgery and overall fitness.

### Can I do cardiac rehab exercises at home?

Yes — walking, ankle pumps, seated marching, deep breathing and sit-to-stand can all be done safely at home once your doctor clears them. Stationary cycling and any resistance work should be introduced only on your team's advice. Periodic follow-up tests such as an [ECG, 2D Echo or TMT](/services/ecg-echo/) help your cardiologist track how your heart is responding.

### What should I avoid after open heart surgery?

Avoid heavy lifting, pushing or pulling, and strenuous upper-body exercise until your surgeon approves it — the breastbone typically needs several weeks to heal. Also avoid exercising in extreme heat, skipping warm-ups and cool-downs, or pushing through chest discomfort or breathlessness.

### Is it normal to feel tired during cardiac rehabilitation?

Mild tiredness after activity is common in the early weeks and usually improves steadily. Unusual or severe fatigue, breathlessness out of proportion to the effort, or new swelling in the legs should be reported to your cardiologist, as they can be signs of [heart failure](/services/heart-failure/) or other complications that need attention.
