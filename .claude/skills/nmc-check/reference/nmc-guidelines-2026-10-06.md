# NMC Guidelines on Ethical Advertising and Public Communication — clause digest

**Source:** National Medical Commission, Ethics & Medical Registration Board — Public Notice No. **R-13014/01/2024-Ethics**, dated **06-10-2026**, signed by the Secretary, NMC. Framed in view of W.P.(C) 1160/2023 (Supreme Court) and the IMC (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002. **In force with immediate effect.**

This is a working paraphrase for review purposes, keyed to the notice's own clause numbers. Short quoted phrases are the notice's own illustrative terms. If wording matters for a decision, check the original PDF.

Abbreviations: **RMP** = Registered Medical Practitioner · **SMC** = State Medical Council · **SMR/NMR** = State / National Medical Register · **CEA** = Clinical Establishments Act.

---

## 1–2. Title, commencement, objectives

- **2.1** Purpose: advertising and digital communication about medical practice must be ethical, truthful, transparent and responsible. Aims: (b) prevent false, misleading, exaggerated, deceptive or unsubstantiated claims; information must be factual, accurate, transparent and verifiable; (c) prevent direct or indirect solicitation and commercial promotion that may induce unnecessary investigations, treatments or procedures; (d) cover digital and emerging technology; (e) uphold patient autonomy, privacy and the dignity of the profession.
- **2.2** Nothing here permits what other law or the 2002 Regulations prohibit.

## 3. Definitions

- **3.2 Advertisement** — any communication, promotion, publicity, announcement, representation or dissemination of information, in any medium, that directly or indirectly solicits or promotes (or is intended to promote) the services, reputation, skills, qualifications, achievements or facilities of an RMP or hospital. Digital, social-media, sponsored, targeted, influencer-led, search-engine, platform-based, audio-visual and AI-generated/assisted communications count when their content or manner of dissemination is promotional.
  - **Explanation I** — ordinary, non-promotional discharge of information to the public without commercial intent is outside the definition.
  - **Explanation II** — availability/installation of equipment, technology, diagnostic facilities or services may be stated as fact, but with no claims of superiority, guaranteed accuracy, guaranteed outcomes, comparison, or anything misleading or unverifiable.
  - **Explanation III** — an RMP publishing any electronic media post should disclose **name, qualifications, registration status and SMR/NMR registration number**.
  - **Explanation IV** — a clinical establishment publishing electronic media posts should disclose the names of its RMPs with qualifications, registration status and registration number.
  - **Explanation V** — no soliciting, purchasing, procuring, manipulating or publishing fake, paid or misleading patient reviews, ratings, testimonials, endorsements or recommendations. An RMP shall not **request or share** patient testimonials, recommendations, endorsements or reviews for professional promotion on social media.
- **3.3 AI** — a self-learning automated process/program/machine doing tasks that usually need a human brain.
- **3.4 Hospital/Medical Institution** — a clinical establishment for diagnosis, treatment or care, in-patient or out-patient.
- **3.5 Medium of advertisement** — includes print, TV, radio, social media, **websites and blogs**, messaging platforms, podcasts, influencer marketing, digital campaigns, sponsored posts, AI-generated promotion, public interviews, news articles intended for promotion, online healthcare platforms, any audio-visual or digital communication.
- **3.6 Social media** — all publication and sharing through internet-based websites, apps etc. that let users create, share and join online communities or network.
- **3.7 RMP** — practitioner on the National Register or a State Medical Register.

## 4. Scope

- **4.1** Applies to RMPs' advertising, publicity, digital communication and promotional activity.
- **4.2** Read with the IMC Regulations 2002 and NMC Act 2019; statute and operative regulations prevail in an irreconcilable conflict.
- **4.3** For hospitals, read with the applicable CEA / State Act, which overrides in case of conflict.
- **4.4** Publishing through a platform, third party, agency, influencer or other intermediary does not by itself absolve the RMP/institution for content it authorised, commissioned, sponsored, adopted or knowingly permitted.

## 5. Public awareness / health education

- **5.1** Practitioners may take part in awareness programmes, public-health campaigns, academic discussion and educational content **provided** it does not promote personal practice, does not solicit patients, and is not monetised through promotional marketing.
- **5.2** Hoardings in public space may be used only for factual information (infrastructure, facilities). Unverified/unverifiable claims are treated as commercial and prohibited.

## 6. Patient privacy and consent

- **6.1** Patient information, photographs, videos, records, clinical images, treatment details, testimonials or other identifiable information may not be used for advertising except where law permits and consent and safeguards are complied with.
- **6.2** **Consent alone does not legitimise** a testimonial, endorsement, promotional patient story, before-and-after depiction, success claim or other practice otherwise prohibited.
- **6.3** Where consent is required it must be specific, informed, voluntary, documented and verifiable, and comply with privacy/data-protection law.
- **6.4** Even where disclosure is lawful, avoid unnecessary disclosure of names; crop, blur or black out birth marks, identification marks or anything identifying.

## 7. Prohibited practices (Chapter II)

- **7.1** Use social media responsibly, balancing public information with patient integrity and professional ethics.
- **7.2** **AI-generated promotional campaigns for commercial interest are prohibited.** Any AI-generated promotional content that otherwise conforms **must carry a source mark explicitly stating its origin is AI.**
  - **(a)** AI must not be used to create or spread misleading, deceptive or unverifiable representations about diagnosis, treatment, outcomes, qualifications, patient experiences or services.
  - **(b)** AI must not be used to create or manipulate a patient's image, testimonial, voice or clinical outcome, or to create a synthetic endorsement or representation purporting to be an actual patient, RMP or other person.
  - **(c)** Where AI-generated or materially AI-altered content's artificial nature may be material to the audience's understanding, appropriate disclosure is required.
  - **(d)** Patient information used as AI input must comply with privacy, confidentiality, data-protection and ethics requirements.
- **7.3** Prohibited practices on advertising, solicitation, endorsements, testimonials, patient images, misleading claims and digital manipulation are governed by Chapter III. **Explanation:** Government of India / State Government programme campaigns in the larger public interest are exempt.
- **7.4** No sharing of patient data (names, faces, anomalies etc.) that can be identified publicly.
- **7.5** No live broadcast or commercial dissemination of surgical/clinical procedures inconsistent with NMC live-surgery guidelines.
- **7.6** Any processing/publication/storage/disclosure of digital personal data must comply with the IT Act 2000 and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules (2025).

## 8.1 Restrictions on RMPs (Chapter III)

- **i.** No advertising aimed at creating unnecessary demand for procedures, promoting unnecessary diagnostics, or fear-based marketing.
- **ii.** No direct or indirect solicitation; no self-promotion of professional services; no engaging third parties to market medical services; no permitting use of name, image, voice, testimonial or endorsement to promote services or products; no campaigns giving an impression of commercialised practice.
- **iii.** No advertising of personal achievements, professional success rates or number of patients treated, with assertions such as "Guaranteed cures", "Best doctors", "No.1 Specialist", "100% success", "painless treatment", "Miracle treatment", "Exclusive cure", or any other unverifiable or exaggerated claim.
- **iv.** No approval, recommendation, endorsement, certificate, report or statement about any drug, medicine, remedy, device, diagnostic product, health product or commercial product for use in advertising in connection with the RMP's name, signature, photograph, voice or status — paid or unpaid.
- **v.** No publishing cases for promotion, advertising surgical results, "before and after" photographs, publicising celebrity patients, or promoting personal success stories — **unless** published strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymised patient consent.
- **vi.** No statement that creates unrealistic expectations, misrepresents outcomes, conceals risks, or promotes unproven therapies, secret remedies or treatments whose composition, scientific basis, safety or efficacy is not adequately established.
- **vii.** No commission, rebate, bonus, gift, referral fee, lead-generation fee or other consideration tied to referral, recommendation or procurement of patients or specimens.
- **viii.** Agency / digital-marketing / influencer / platform engagements must not be structured so payment is linked to procuring or referring individual patients.
- **ix.** No comparative claims — "best", "No.1", "leading", "most trusted", "top", "number one", "unmatched" or similar — unless objectively verifiable, based on a transparent and independently ascertainable methodology, and otherwise lawful. *Proviso:* any ranking or award relied on must be independently verifiable, with material conditions, methodology, date and awarding organisation disclosed where needed to avoid misleading.
- **x.** No discounts, limited-period offers, contests, coupons, gifts, cashbacks, referral benefits, free procedures or similar inducements likely to encourage unnecessary consultation, investigation or treatment or amounting to solicitation. *Any lawful disclosure of charges, packages or fees must be factual, transparent and not misleading.*
- **xi.** No using or permitting any celebrity, influencer, patient, employee or third party to promote services through prohibited testimonials, endorsements or recommendations. Material commercial relationships in lawful public-health/informational communication must be disclosed. No doing indirectly through a third party what is prohibited directly.
- **xii.** No procuring or manipulating fake followers, likes, reviews, ratings, testimonials, comments, views or other engagement, **or manipulating search rankings, visibility or algorithms**, to create a misleading impression of professional standing.

## 8.2 Exceptions for RMPs

- **i.** Research-based communication only — case studies published in reputed medical journals.
- **ii.** Formal announcements: (a) starting practice, (b) change of type of practice, (c) change of address, (d) temporary absence, (e) resumption of another practice, (f) succeeding to another practice, (g) public declaration of charges.
- **iii.** Lectures/talks on public health and diseases via TV, radio and electronic media in the practitioner's own name and designation, without promoting the employer organisation.
- **iv.** Health-education and general-information pamphlets/brochures for patients visiting the clinic — no guarantee of cure, no superiority over others, no unsubstantiated or misleading content.
- **v.** Greetings on days of national importance or related to the profession — no advertisement of professional gain.

## 8.3 Hospital / medical institution advertising

- **i.** May disseminate factual, objective, verifiable information: name, location, contact details, departments, facilities, equipment, services, emergency services, accreditation status, charges and other institutional information permitted by law.
- **ii.** Must not contain patient solicitation, inducement, comparative superiority claims, guarantees of cure or outcome, unverifiable or exaggerated claims, or **promotional portrayal of an individual RMP**. The form, medium or frequency of communication must not turn otherwise factual information into solicitation or self-aggrandisement.
- **iii.** Where advertising is permitted by law, stay within that law and these Guidelines.

## 9. Do's and don'ts (Chapter IV)

- **9.1** Establishments may advertise only factual, objective, verifiable particulars: name, contact details, departments, diagnostic facilities, emergency services, accreditation status, other facilities and applicable fees. A hospital website's **directory of its doctors** — names, recognised qualifications, specialties, registration details, availability — serving a patient-information function is materially distinct from a paid or promotional advertisement or inducement.
- **9.2** Use of an RMP's photograph/image is not permitted where it constitutes self-advertisement, solicitation or promotional publicity. Self-photographs on letterheads or signboards are governed by Regulations 6.1.1 and 6.1.2 of the 2002 Regulations and regarded as unethical.
- **9.3** Formal announcements must be factual, non-promotional and limited in scope: starting practice, change of address, change of practice type, temporary absence, resumption, public declaration of consultation fees.
- **9.4** Healthcare platforms hosting RMPs must comply with NMC norms, must not offer paid ranking of doctors, and must be transparent in listing practices.

## 10. Penalties (Chapter V)

- **10.1** Graded action the SMC may contemplate against the RMP:
  1. First violation — warning and mandatory ethics training
  2. Second — censure and monetary penalty
  3. Third — suspension of registration, 3–6 months
  4. Serious violation (misleading cure claims, inducement of patients, digital mass solicitation etc.) — suspension, 6–12 months
  5. Repeated — removal from the register, 1–3 years

  Hospitals are dealt with under the applicable CEA / State Act.
- **10.2** Penalties only after a show-cause notice and an opportunity to explain.
- **10.3** The SMC must issue a reasoned, speaking order.

## 11. Appeals

- **11.1** Appeal to EMRB, NMC within 60 days (s. 30(3) NMC Act). **11.4** Second appeal under s. 30(4) within 60 days. **11.5** For hospital violations the CEA mechanism applies; an RMP who personally authorised, participated in or endorsed the communication may be examined separately.

## 12. Clarifications

NMC may issue clarifications, advisories, SOPs or procedural instructions. Check for these before relying on this digest for a borderline call.

## Interpretation notes (reviewer's, not the NMC's)

- **8.1.iii is ambiguously drafted.** It can be read as (a) a ban on advertising achievements, success rates and patient numbers at all, or (b) a ban on doing so *with* exaggerated assertions. The conservative reading is (a) for promotional placements.
- **Explanation V's last sentence refers to "social media"**, which 3.6 defines broadly. Whether a clinic's own static website falls inside is arguable; 8.1.ii, 8.1.xi and 6.2 reach testimonials used for promotion regardless of medium.
- **8.2.i vs 8.1.v.** 8.1.v allows cases "strictly for scientific or educational purposes with anonymized patient consent"; 8.2.i says only journal-published research communication is allowed. A website case study is safest when it is anonymised, consented, educational in substance and free of promotional framing — and remains an open question to raise with the doctor.
- **7.2's first two sentences sit in tension** (AI promotional campaigns prohibited; conforming AI promotional content must carry a source mark). Practical position: label all AI-generated or materially AI-altered content, and never use AI to depict people, outcomes or endorsements.
